### 4. nEye.ai

**Vertical:** CRISP | **Total Raised to Date:** $152M | **Stage:** Series C | **NVIDIA Tech Alignment:** 🟢 Strong potential

**What we do:** nEye.ai has built an optical circuit switch (OCS) on a single chip — combining silicon photonics, MEMS, and CMOS into a foundry-ready chip that replaces the large, expensive mechanical optical switching systems that currently limit AI data center interconnect bandwidth. In large GPU clusters, optical circuit switching enables dynamic reconfiguration of high-bandwidth GPU-to-GPU interconnects, allowing the network topology to adapt in real time to workload demands rather than remaining statically wired. Founded 2020 by UC Berkeley PhDs Tae Joon Seok and Kyungmok Mogi Kwon.

**Today's interesting [article](https://techstartups.com/2026/04/14/neye-ai-raises-80m-to-scale-optical-circuit-switching-for-ai-data-centers/):** nEye.ai raised an $80M Series C on April 14 led by Sutter Hill Ventures, with CapitalG (Alphabet's independent growth fund), M12 (Microsoft's venture fund), Socratic Partners, and Micron Technology all participating. The investor composition — Alphabet's growth fund and Microsoft's venture arm together, alongside a silicon supply chain player (Micron) and a deep infrastructure VC (Sutter Hill) — is an unusually strong signal of conviction from companies that build and operate GPU-scale data centers.

**Investors:** Sutter Hill Ventures (lead), CapitalG (Alphabet), M12 (Microsoft), Socratic Partners, Micron Technology

**NVIDIA Relevancy:** Optical circuit switching is the networking fabric that connects NVIDIA GPU clusters. As GPU pod sizes scale toward NVL72 configurations requiring 130 TB/s NVLink bandwidth, the external switching fabric between pods becomes a critical bottleneck — a problem nEye.ai's OCS-on-chip technology directly addresses. This is complementary infrastructure to NVIDIA's Spectrum-X Ethernet for AI networking; large-scale InfiniBand and Spectrum-X deployments would benefit from the reconfigurability that optical switching enables between GPU nodes.

**Inception Status:** Not in Inception

**Website:** [neye.ai](https://neye.ai)

## Interesting Trends

*Three macro signals emerging from today's news:*

1. **The agentic internet is collapsing the human/AI distinction in enterprise software** — Mintlify's disclosure that 50%+ of documentation traffic is now AI agents is a first-order signal about how rapidly the web is being reconfigured for machine consumption rather than human reading. Claude Code alone is generating 200M documentation requests per month — meaning AI coding agents have already become the primary consumer of developer documentation at scale. This pattern will propagate across every category of enterprise software: the buyer is human, but the user is increasingly an AI agent. Companies that rebuild their product architecture for agent-first consumption (like Mintlify, Auctor, and Ranger AI in this briefing) are building for the real adoption curve.

2. **Energy economics are pulling quantum computing into AI infrastructure** — Sygaldry Technologies raising $139M represents a new thesis: quantum hardware is no longer being sold as a future computational capability, but as an energy efficiency solution for today's AI training cost problem. Chad Rigetti's framing — "converting megawatts into intelligence more efficiently" — is the AI CFO's problem, not the AI researcher's problem. Breakthrough Energy Ventures leading confirms this is a serious energy-infrastructure bet, not a long-horizon research investment. For NVIDIA, this creates an interesting position: cuQuantum already sits at the interface between GPU compute and quantum simulation, and quantum-GPU hybrid architectures will require NVIDIA's classical compute infrastructure as the execution layer regardless of which quantum hardware approach wins.

3. **European defence tech is entering a rapid commercialization phase** — Stendr's $5.4M round on April 15 and the broader surge in European counter-drone startups (TYTAN Technologies, Sensofusion, and others) reflect a structural change: European governments are now writing procurement contracts for AI-native defence startups at a pace that didn't exist two years ago. The Ukraine conflict has compressed the typical decade-long defence procurement cycle into months for specific use cases (drone detection, counter-UAS, battlefield AI). NVIDIA's Jetson Orin ecosystem and sovereign NIM deployments are the natural infrastructure play for this wave — these systems cannot route through US cloud providers and require edge-capable, air-gappable compute.
