## Interesting Trends

1. **SEA's clearest NVIDIA-native bets are physical and infrastructural, not app-layer** — The five 🟢 companies (Dyna.Ai, LionsBot, NuSpace, Valiance Health, SILITH) all sit at a compute or hardware layer: deployed agentic AI in banks, a 6,000-robot AMR fleet, an on-orbit IoT constellation, a clinical-data-engineering pipeline, and silicon-photonics interconnects for AI data centers. The five 🟠 companies are vertical SaaS/services (pharmacy, HR, telehealth, proptech, edtech) adding AI layers on proprietary data — real businesses, but software-only with no meaningful compute yet. For Inception, the split is a clean triage line: engage the infrastructure layer now, watch the SaaS layer for when its AI roadmap becomes compute.

2. **The AI-datacenter supply chain is quietly forming in Singapore** — SILITH's UMC mass-production milestone (silicon-photonics interconnects, 1.6T platform) lands the same week as broader SEA AI-infrastructure buildout. SILITH is complementary to NVIDIA's networking roadmap rather than built on it — a strategic-adjacency account where the software touchpoint is CUDA-X-accelerated EDA (Synopsys pattern), not GPUs. Singapore is becoming an optical-interconnect design hub worth mapping alongside its data-center capacity.

3. **"AI-enabled vertical SaaS" is the dominant SEA startup shape — and the region's proprietary-data moats are underexploited** — Arincare (6,000 pharmacies), BrioHR (1,000+ SMEs), Horganice (฿6B rent flow, 1M+ rooms), and &you (100,000+ health assessments) each sit on distinctive transaction/behavioral data but run generic software stacks. The recurring pattern — forecasting, copilots, risk scoring, personalization — maps repeatedly to RAPIDS, NIM, and Merlin. NVIDIA's ecosystem opportunity here is developer-relations breadth (RAPIDS/NIM adoption in vertical SaaS) rather than large training deals.
