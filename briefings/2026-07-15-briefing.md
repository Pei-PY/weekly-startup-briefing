## Interesting Trends

1. **Sovereign, cost-efficient inference is SEA's new deep-tech wedge** — Sapiens AI (distillation + RL for 90%-at-10%-cost LLMs) and SnapEdit (bootstrapped GenAI image models at 10M+ users) are both winning on *inference economics*, not model novelty. The common thread is that owning the serving stack — where TensorRT-LLM, Triton/Dynamo, and NIM live — is the difference between margin and API dependency. This is the clearest signal yet that SEA's most GPU-native companies are optimizing the same efficiency curve NVIDIA's software stack is built to bend.

2. **Physical-world data platforms are maturing from logging to prediction** — Red Dot Analytics (data-center digital twins), JALA (aquaculture disease forecasting), and Duon (predictive foot traffic) all raised on the promise of *forecasting*, not dashboards. Each converts sensor/telemetry streams into physics-ML or time-series prediction — Omniverse/PhysicsNeMo, RAPIDS cuML, and Metropolis respectively — the exact workloads where the acceleration gap is largest and most defensible.

3. **SEA fintech's AI story is still overwhelmingly a risk-modeling story** — Three of today's ten (PasarPolis, iNexus, BillEase) are fintechs whose NVIDIA fit routes entirely through RAPIDS-class credit/fraud modeling and Morpheus-class detection, all mapped to the same bunq reference. The pattern: SEA lenders and insurers remain CPU-bound on their highest-value workload (underwriting), making them consistent — if software-only (🟠) — Inception candidates whose acceleration case is retraining frequency and fraud precision, not net-new GPU-native product.
